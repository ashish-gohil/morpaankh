# Morpaankh Brand Lexicon

The word-bank to name from, the words to avoid, and worked examples. Pull the **field that matches the product's
hook**, then generate fresh inside it — don't just lift the first seed word.

> **Before you finalise any non-English name, confirm its exact meaning and that it carries no awkward or off
> connotation for a women's garment.** The senses below are the common poetic ones. The words are drawn from the
> shared Hindustani poetic vocabulary (some Sanskrit/Hindi, some Persian/Urdu) and all read as _pan-Indian
> premium_, not regional. Avoid anything that sounds specifically **Gujarati** (a standing brand rule), and avoid
> a word that is mainly a living person's first name _unless_ you're using the person-named sub-line (see end).
>
> **Also confirm uniqueness and trademark safety** before locking a name (see "Uniqueness & trademark safety" in
> SKILL.md): no repeats in the catalogue, and don't pick a word that's already another clothing brand's name, a
> famous mark, or a film/song/celebrity title. Common words used decoratively (most of this list) are the safe
> default; for a recurring collection name, run the IP India Wordmark + Phonetic search under Class 25 first.

## Word-bank by field

**Light & radiance** — Noor (light/radiance, Urdu), Roshni (light, Hindi), Chandni (moonlight, Hindi), Sitara

Hindi), Subah (morning, Urdu/Hindi), Raat (night, Hindi).

**Garden & flora in bloom** ("where stories bloom") — Bageecha (garden, Hindi), Gulzar (a garden in bloom, Urdu),
Gulnaar (pomegranate flower, Persian), Nargis (narcissus, Persian), Kali (a flower bud, Hindi), Bela (jasmine, Hindi),
Juhi (a jasmine variety, Hindi), Champa (frangipani, Hindi), Suman (flower, Sanskrit), Vatika (garden, Sanskrit),
Kusum (flower, Sanskrit).

**Celebration & courtly life** — Jashn (celebration, Urdu), Utsav (festival, Sanskrit), Mehfil (an evening gathering/

**Colour, dye & craft** — Kesar (saffron, Hindi), Sona (gold, Hindi), Rangat (hue/complexion, Urdu), Rangrez (the

**Soul & feeling** — Roohi / Ruhi (of the soul, Urdu), Suroor (a gentle euphoria, Urdu), Khwaab (dream, Urdu),

## Name-shape patterns (one to three words)

Most names are a single word from the word-bank above. For a two- or three-word name, use one of these two shapes
only. Both must still read in one breath and stay easy to spell.

**The "-e-" link (izafat).** Joins two words into a flowing "[A]-e-[B]" = "A of B." The most premium and most
distinctly Indian multi-word form. Frame: **[element / light / colour]-e-[mood / season / place].** Build fresh
pairings from the word-bank; keep both halves short.

- Light & dusk: _Noor-e-Saanjh_ (light of dusk), _Noor-e-Sahar_ (light of dawn), _Rang-e-Saanjh_ (colour of dusk).
- Gathering & night: _Shab-e-Mehfil_ (night of the gathering), _Rang-e-Mehfil_ (colour of the gathering),
  _Bazm-e-Raunak_ (assembly aglow).
- Bloom & story (ties to "where stories bloom"): _Daastan-e-Bahaar_ (a tale of spring), _Gul-e-Sehra_ (flower of
  the wild), _Gul-e-Saawan_ (flower of the monsoon).
- Verify the phrase reads right to an Urdu/Hindi ear, and **web-check before using it as a recurring collection
  name.** Avoid phrases that are already known film, song, qawwali, or fragrance titles (e.g. _Jashn-e-Bahaar_,
  _Jashn-e-Rekhta_, _Shab-e-Maikhana_); coin a fresh combination instead.

**The two-word pair (no link word).** Two short word-bank words whose combined meaning is real and which sound
better together than apart. _Mor Saanjh_ (peacock dusk), _Saawan Noor_ (monsoon light), _Sona Megh_ (golden cloud),
_Neel Saanjh_ (blue dusk), _Megh Malhar_ is a raga so avoid as a plain name. Drop one word if it sounds bolted on.

**Do not** use four+ words, an English adjective on a Hindustani word (_Golden Noor_, _Royal Saanjh_), or a synonym
stack. In the URL handle, write an "-e-" name as plain hyphens (_noor-e-saanjh_).

## Deeper ethnic anchors (the rooted, premium layer)

Use these to make a name or — more often — a **description** feel specifically Indian and crafted. Naming a motif,
a weave, or a rite by its real name is what separates premium ethnic copy from generic prettiness. Verify any term
against the actual product before using it (don't call a print "Ajrakh" unless it is).

**Motifs & patterns** — Buta / Buti (the small floral or paisley motif sprinkled across a fabric), Ambi / Keri /
Kairi (the mango-shaped paisley), Bel (a winding floral vine, usually a border), Jaali (lattice / net-like
openwork), Kalga / Kalka (the large paisley), Gul (flower), Mor (peacock motif), Bagh ("garden" — a densely
flowered field), Lehar (a wave, as in Leheriya). _These also make elegant names (Ambi, Bel, Kalga, Lehar)._

**Crafts, weaves & techniques** — name the real one: **Banarasi** (Varanasi silk brocade), **Chanderi** (sheer
cotton-silk, MP), **Maheshwari** (MP), **Kanjeevaram** (TN silk), **Jamdani** (fine Bengal muslin figuring),
**Bandhej / Bandhani** (tie-dye), **Leheriya** (diagonal wave tie-dye), **Ajrakh** (resist block-print), **Bagru /
Bagh** (block-prints), **Kalamkari** (pen / block narrative print), **Chikankari** (Lucknow white shadow-work),
**Zardozi** (raised metallic embroidery), **Gota Patti** (gold appliqué ribbon-work), **Kantha** (running-stitch),
**Phulkari** (Punjabi floral darning), **Mukaish / Badla** (tiny metal dots). Drop the right one into a description
and the piece reads handmade and considered.

**Indian time, season & raga** — Prahar (a ~3-hour division of the day), Sandhya (twilight, Sanskrit; the root of
Saanjh), the six ritus / seasons — Vasant/Basant (spring), Grishma (summer), Varsha/Sawan (monsoon), Sharad
(autumn), Hemant & Shishir (winter) — and the ragas tied to an hour or season: **Malhar / Megh Malhar** (the
monsoon raga), **Bhairav** (a dawn raga), **Yaman** (an evening raga). Lovely for naming a seasonal capsule.

**Festivals & rites** (for the occasion line) — Teej (the monsoon festival; green, for new brides), Karva Chauth,
Diwali / Deepavali, Navratri, Basant Panchami, Lohri, Eid, and the wedding rites: Haldi, Mehndi/Mehendi, Sangeet,
Vidaai. Anchor "the occasion it's made for" to a real one rather than a vague "any festive occasion".

## Words to avoid (the de-AI blacklist)

These make a name sound generated. Don't use them, and don't use names _like_ them.

- **Abstract English virtues:** Radiance, Serenity, Grace, Elegance, Majesty, Bliss, Aura, Opulence, Allure, Charm,
  Glamour, Vogue, Luxe — belong to any brand, say nothing about the piece.
- **Overused "Sanskrit/Latin-luxe":** Aananda, Nirvana, Aurelia, Seraphina, Elysian, Ethereal, Celestial, Divine,
  Eternal, Mystique, Couture, Heritage, Atelier — generator defaults.
- **Over-literal theme names:** Peacock Grace, Royal Mor, Mor Magic, Festive Bloom, Ethnic Charm, Regal Affair.
- **Rule-of-three / stacks:** "Grace & Glamour", "Tradition & Trend", "Bloom & Bliss", "Style & Soul".
- **Colour/fabric in a costume:** Saffron Dream, Golden Glow, Silk Saga, Emerald Elegance.
- **Spelling traps:** Reyhaanaa, Aaraadhyaa, Khwaahish (use Khwaab), or anything with random extra vowels/diacritics.
- **Generic + garment combos:** "Festive Edit", "The Saffron Set", "Celebration Co-ord".
- **Premium-claiming adjectives** (in names _or_ descriptions): Luxurious, Exquisite, Elegant, Opulent, Regal,
  Royal, Sumptuous, Lavish, Majestic, Stunning, Gorgeous, Glamorous, Premium — these read mass-market. Show the
  craft with specifics instead and let the reader conclude the piece is premium.

Test each survivor: _Would a name generator produce this for any ethnic brand?_ (if yes, cut) and _Does the meaning
say something specific about THIS piece?_ (if no, cut).

## Voice snippets (description tone only)

These show the _tone_ for a single name. The real output for any product is **three** named options, each with its
own description, as set out in SKILL.md → Output format. Note the fabric/craft story in each, and that none uses a long dash.

**Sukoon** (calm, Urdu) — a sage-green block-print cotton kurti:

> Sukoon is the word for an unhurried morning, tea going cold while you finish a thought. This is soft cotton in a
> quiet sage green, printed with a small block buti, the kind of motif Rajasthan's block-printers have worked for
> generations. It breathes on a warm day and softens with every wash. The kurti you reach for when you'd rather
> feel like yourself than dressed up.

**Rimjhim** (the patter of light rain, Hindi) — a sheer ivory organza cloud-print co-ord:

> Rimjhim is the sound the first soft rain makes, the shower the peacocks wait all summer for. The cloth is organza,
> crisp and sheer, the weave that holds its shape and catches light, with grey-blue clouds drifting across the
> ivory. It carries a little air under it when you walk. For a Sawan afternoon, or any day you want the season on
> your side.

**Mayuri** (the peacock herself, Sanskrit) — a teal silk peacock-motif 3-piece:

> Mayuri is the peacock herself. The base is deep teal silk, the feather drawn across it in gold zari with the
> chandrak, the feather's eye, opening along the border. Zari is the fine metal-wrapped thread that has edged
> Banarasi weaves for generations, and here it does the quiet work. This is the motif the whole brand is named
> for, so we let it lead and kept everything else still. _(Reserve peacock-direct names for pieces where the
> peacock truly is the motif.)_

## Fabric & craft stories (for authentic descriptions)

Use these to give a description its premium, authentic ring. **Tell the story of the fabric or craft _type_ only.**
Never claim a specific origin for a given piece (a named village or weaver, "handwoven", "hand-printed", a count of
hours or artisans) unless the vendor's notes actually say so. Match the story to what the vendor states.

Fabrics:

- **French crepe** — smooth, lightweight crepe with a soft matte surface and a fluid fall; the faint grain comes
  from tightly twisted yarns. Drapes close without clinging, holds a flared shape, travels well.
- **Georgette** — a sheer, lightweight crepe with a gentle bounce and flow; named after the early-1900s French
  dressmaker Georgette de la Plante. Moves a lot, photographs soft.
- **Organza** — sheer, crisp, lightweight plain weave; holds structure and volume and catches light. Gives shape
  to a sleeve, a flare, a dupatta.
- **Chanderi** — a fine, lightweight handloom cloth from Chanderi, Madhya Pradesh, known for a soft sheen and a
  near-translucent body; a silk-cotton blend that feels airy and a little glossy.
- **Silk / Banarasi** — silk carries weight, sheen, and a sense of occasion; "Banarasi" is the brocade weaving
  tradition of Varanasi, usually worked with zari.
- **Cotton / mulmul** — breathable and everyday; mulmul (fine muslin) is soft and light and gets better with washing.
- **Modal, micro silk, inner lining** — a smooth lining that lets the outer cloth move; mention only as a "soft
  inner lining", never as a selling feature.

Crafts & techniques:

- **Shisha / mirror-work (abhla)** — small mirrors stitched onto cloth and framed in thread; a Gujarat and
  Rajasthan tradition once made with mica, believed to catch light and turn away the evil eye, later set with glass.
- **Zari** — fine metal-wrapped thread (historically gold or silver) woven or embroidered into motifs; the soul of Banarasi work.
- **Zardozi** — raised metallic-thread embroidery with Persian roots that flourished in the Mughal courts, worked over a frame.
- **Resham / aari** — silk-thread embroidery; aari is the hooked-needle technique that makes fine chain-stitch motifs.
- **Chikankari** — delicate white shadow-embroidery from Lucknow, a Mughal-era craft, traditionally white on white.
- **Bandhej / bandhani** — tie-dye from Gujarat and Rajasthan, thousands of tiny points tied by hand before dyeing.
- **Block print (Bagru, Sanganeri, Ajrakh)** — patterns stamped with hand-carved wooden blocks; Ajrakh (Kutch) uses
  layered natural-dye resist printing.
- **Gota patti** — Rajasthani appliqué in gold or silver ribbon, edged onto the cloth.

## Optional: a person-named sub-line (the Suta lever)

Suta names pieces after people, and customers buy pieces that share their own name — a real, repeatable driver.
Morpaankh can run a small sub-line of pieces named as **short, easy women's names** (Mira, Naina, Zoya, Roohi,
Amaira, Sana, Tara) for that effect, while the house default stays on the evocative peacock-world words above.
If you do this, keep the names short, widely loved across India, and unmistakably first-names so the intent is
clear. Avoid any name strongly tied to a specific celebrity or public figure, and still give each a one-line story.
